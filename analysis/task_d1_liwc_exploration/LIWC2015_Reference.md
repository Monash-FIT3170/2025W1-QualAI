# LIWC2015 Framework Reference

## Overview

The Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count (LIWC) is a text analysis program that counts words in psychologically meaningful categories. LIWC2015 is the latest version developed by James W. Pennebaker and colleagues at the University of Texas at Austin.

## LIWC2015 Variable Categories

### Summary Variables (4 variables)

These composite scores provide high-level insights into writing style:

1. **Analytic** (0-100 scale)
   - Measures formal, logical, and hierarchical thinking
   - Higher scores = more analytical thinking
   - Lower scores = more narrative, personal, here-and-now thinking
   - Based on function words (articles, prepositions, etc.)

2. **Clout** (0-100 scale)
   - Measures relative social status, confidence, and leadership
   - Higher scores = more confident, higher status
   - Lower scores = more tentative, lower status
   - Based on pronouns and verb forms

3. **Authentic** (0-100 scale)
   - Measures personal authenticity and honesty
   - Higher scores = more personal, humble, vulnerable
   - Lower scores = more guarded, distanced
   - Based on multiple linguistic markers

4. **Tone** (0-100 scale)
   - Measures emotional tone/positivity
   - Higher scores = more positive, upbeat
   - Lower scores = more negative, sad, anxious
   - Based on positive and negative emotion words

### Linguistic Dimensions (~45 variables)

#### General Descriptors
- **WC**: Word count
- **WPS**: Words per sentence
- **Sixltr**: Words > 6 letters (%)
- **Dic**: Dictionary words (%)

#### Function Words
- **function**: Total function words
- **pronoun**: Total pronouns
- **ppron**: Personal pronouns (I, we, you, they)
- **i**: First person singular (I, me, my)
- **we**: First person plural (we, us, our)
- **you**: Second person (you, your)
- **shehe**: Third person singular (she, he, him, her)
- **they**: Third person plural (they, them, their)
- **ipron**: Impersonal pronouns (it, those)
- **article**: Articles (a, an, the)
- **prep**: Prepositions (to, with, above)
- **auxverb**: Auxiliary verbs (am, will, have)
- **adverb**: Adverbs (very, really)
- **conj**: Conjunctions (and, but, whereas)
- **negate**: Negations (no, not, never)

#### Other Grammar
- **verb**: Regular verbs
- **adj**: Adjectives
- **compare**: Comparatives (greater, best, after)
- **interrog**: Interrogatives (how, when, what)
- **number**: Numbers
- **quant**: Quantifiers (few, many, much)

### Psychological Processes (~41 variables)

#### Affective Processes
- **affect**: Total affective processes
- **posemo**: Positive emotions (love, nice, sweet)
- **negemo**: Negative emotions
  - **anx**: Anxiety (worried, fearful)
  - **anger**: Anger (hate, kill, annoyed)
  - **sad**: Sadness (crying, grief, sad)

#### Social Processes
- **social**: Total social processes
- **family**: Family (daughter, dad, aunt)
- **friend**: Friends (buddy, neighbor)
- **female**: Female references (girl, her, mom)
- **male**: Male references (boy, his, dad)

#### Cognitive Processes
- **cogproc**: Total cognitive processes
- **insight**: Insight (think, know, consider)
- **cause**: Causation (because, effect, hence)
- **discrep**: Discrepancy (should, would, could)
- **tentat**: Tentative (maybe, perhaps, guess)
- **certain**: Certainty (always, never)
- **differ**: Differentiation (hasn't, but, else)

#### Perceptual Processes
- **percept**: Total perceptual processes
- **see**: See (view, saw, seen)
- **hear**: Hear (listen, hearing)
- **feel**: Feel (feels, touch)

#### Biological Processes
- **bio**: Total biological processes
- **body**: Body (cheek, hands, spit)
- **health**: Health (clinic, flu, pill)
- **sexual**: Sexuality (horny, love, incest)
- **ingest**: Ingestion (dish, eat, pizza)

#### Drives
- **drives**: Total drives
- **affiliation**: Affiliation (ally, friend, social)
- **achieve**: Achievement (win, success, better)
- **power**: Power (superior, bully)
- **reward**: Reward (take, prize, benefit)
- **risk**: Risk (danger, doubt)

#### Time Orientations
- **focuspast**: Past focus (ago, did, talked)
- **focuspresent**: Present focus (today, is, now)
- **focusfuture**: Future focus (may, will, soon)

#### Relativity
- **relativ**: Total relativity
- **motion**: Motion (arrive, car, go)
- **space**: Space (down, in, thin)
- **time**: Time (end, until, season)

### Personal Concerns (~8 variables)
- **work**: Work (job, majors, xerox)
- **leisure**: Leisure (cook, chat, movie)
- **home**: Home (apartment, kitchen, family)
- **money**: Money (audit, cash, owe)
- **relig**: Religion (altar, church, mosque)
- **death**: Death (bury, coffin, kill)

### Informal Language (Optional)
- **swear**: Swear words
- **netspeak**: Internet slang (btw, lol, thx)
- **assent**: Assent (agree, OK, yes)
- **nonflu**: Nonfluencies (er, hm, umm)
- **filler**: Fillers (blah, Imean, youknow)

### Punctuation (Optional)
- **AllPunc**: Total punctuation
- **Period**: Periods
- **Comma**: Commas
- **Colon**: Colons
- **SemiC**: Semicolons
- **QMark**: Question marks
- **Exclam**: Exclamation marks
- **Dash**: Dashes
- **Quote**: Quotation marks
- **Apostro**: Apostrophes
- **Parenth**: Parentheses
- **OtherP**: Other punctuation

## Interpreting LIWC Scores

### Summary Variables Interpretation

**Analytic Thinking (High scores 70+)**
- More formal, logical reasoning
- More hierarchical thinking
- Common in academic, technical writing
- Associated with STEM disciplines

**Analytic Thinking (Low scores <50)**
- More narrative, personal
- More dynamic, in-the-moment
- Common in personal narratives
- Associated with humanities, creative writing

**Clout (High scores 70+)**
- High social status/confidence
- Leadership language
- Expert positioning

**Clout (Low scores <50)**
- Lower social status
- More tentative, questioning
- Learner positioning

**Authenticity (High scores 70+)**
- More personal, honest
- Self-revealing
- Emotionally immediate

**Authenticity (Low scores <50)**
- More guarded, formal
- Emotional distance
- Careful self-presentation

**Tone (High scores 70+)**
- Positive, upbeat
- Optimistic

**Tone (Low scores <50)**
- Negative, anxious, sad
- Pessimistic

### Function Words and Cognitive Style

**Pronouns**:
- **I/me**: Self-focus, personal narrative, lower status
- **We**: Group focus, inclusiveness
- **You**: Other-focus, engagement
- **They**: Distancing, objectivity

**Articles (a, an, the)**:
- Higher use = more concrete, categorical thinking
- Part of analytic thinking score

**Prepositions**:
- Higher use = more complex, precise thinking
- Part of analytic thinking score

### Psychological Processes

**Positive Emotions**:
- General indicator of well-being
- Can indicate engagement, satisfaction

**Negative Emotions**:
- May indicate distress, conflict
- Subcategories (anxiety, anger, sadness) provide specificity

**Cognitive Processes**:
- High insight words = introspection, understanding
- High causation = logical reasoning
- High discrepancy = problem-solving focus
- High tentative = uncertainty, exploration
- High certainty = confidence, dogmatism

**Social Processes**:
- Indicates social orientation
- Specific subcategories reveal relationship focus

### Temporal Orientation

- **Past focus**: Reflection, nostalgia, storytelling
- **Present focus**: Immediacy, engagement
- **Future focus**: Planning, goals, anticipation

## Research Applications

### Educational Context (Relevant to This Task)

1. **Disciplinary Differences**
   - STEM vs. Humanities writing styles
   - Analytic thinking typically higher in STEM
   - Personal concerns vary by discipline

2. **Gender Communication**
   - Well-documented differences in social language
   - Women often use more social, affective language
   - Men often use more analytical, achievement language
   - Important: Within-group variation > between-group differences

3. **Student Engagement**
   - High "we" suggests community engagement
   - High "I" may indicate personal investment
   - Cognitive processes indicate thinking depth

### Effect Size Guidelines

**Cohen's d** (for t-tests):
- 0.2 = small effect
- 0.5 = medium effect
- 0.8 = large effect

**Eta-squared (η²)** (for ANOVA):
- 0.01 = small effect
- 0.06 = medium effect
- 0.14 = large effect

## Common Pitfalls and Considerations

### 1. Context Matters
- LIWC scores must be interpreted in context
- What's "high" or "low" depends on genre, situation
- Compare within similar contexts

### 2. Multiple Testing
- Testing many variables increases false positive risk
- Use corrections (Bonferroni, FDR)
- Focus on effect sizes, not just p-values

### 3. Linguistic Limitations
- LIWC is dictionary-based, not context-aware
- Cannot detect sarcasm, irony
- Works best with >50 words per document

### 4. Not All Categories Are Relevant
- Choose categories based on research questions
- Some categories may have low base rates
- Consider zero-inflation in analyses

### 5. Correlation ≠ Causation
- LIWC reveals associations, not causes
- Multiple interpretations possible
- Triangulate with other evidence
